Who really builds a building? Not just the crew with the hammers. It starts with one owner, one empty plot, and one big idea.
Most people think design is just pretty drawings. But every line has a price: the designer draws it, and the QS counts its cost.
Then come the hidden gates: government approvals, and a tender where contractors compete. And the lowest price is not always the best value.
On site, the contractor builds, while the supervision consultant checks every pour and every bolt against the approved drawings.
Here is the truth: a project never succeeds because of one hero. It succeeds when six parties keep their promises to each other.
Inspections pass, the keys change hands, and an idea becomes a place for people. Which role matters most? Tell us in the comments.
