In some places, Earth's inner heat does not stay hidden. It rises to the surface and bakes the rock underfoot. Water trapped in the ground is heated far past boiling point, until it bursts out as jets of scalding steam. In the hottest places, even the mud begins to bubble. The most famous of these jets is Old Faithful, in Yellowstone National Park in the United States. It shoots hot water and steam up to 55 metres into the air, roughly every hour and a half. The wait is not random: that is how long it takes for the chamber beneath to fill with water and heat up again. Yellowstone is a land of extremes. It holds around half of all the geysers on Earth, along with thousands of hot springs, mud pots and steaming vents. But all of this sits on top of something far more powerful: a supervolcano. The whole region lies inside an enormous caldera, a collapsed volcanic crater tens of kilometres across. Ordinary volcanoes build mountains. Supervolcanoes blow them apart. Yellowstone's last giant eruption, about 640,000 years ago, was roughly a thousand times more powerful than the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens. Underground, the water still reaches more than 200 degrees Celsius, and new geysers, mud pools and steam vents keep appearing. The tallest geyser ever seen lived for only four years. After a volcanic eruption in New Zealand, the Waimangu Geyser began erupting in 1900. Its jets of black water, rock and steam rose as high as 460 metres, taller than the Empire State Building. In 1903, a sudden, violent eruption swept away four tourists who had come too close. Not every hot spring is dangerous. In the mountains of Nagano, Japan, warm pools fed by hot springs have been adopted by an unlikely set of bathers: Japanese macaque monkeys. Through the freezing winter, they soak in these natural hot tubs, and some get so comfortable they doze off in the water. And at Pamukkale in Turkey, mineral-rich hot water pours down a hillside. As the water cools, its minerals turn to stone, building gleaming white terraces filled with warm blue pools. Its name means "Cotton Castle", and it is one of the most beautiful places on Earth.
