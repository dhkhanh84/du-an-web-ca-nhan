## FULL VOICEOVER (single take)
In 1993, investors told him video games weren't a market. His mother asked why he didn't just get a job.
Sixteen years later, at Stanford, Jensen Huang explained how he got past that. Lesson one: don't wait for a grand vision. Everyone has a perspective. That's all a vision really is.
His perspective in 1993: take 3D graphics off expensive workstations, make them cheap, and let video games pull the whole PC market forward.
Lesson two: sometimes you go against your customers. Dell, HP, IBM and Gateway all said it cost too much. He still bet the company's last few million on a chip that was far too big, sure the technology would get cheap fast.
About two hundred companies went after 3D graphics. Money, talent and technology were roughly equal. What decided who survived, he said, was understanding why your business works.
Lesson three: get comfortable with ambiguity. Leaders don't have to be outgoing. They do have to act before the outcome is clear.
Lesson four: tear it down and rebuild. Every success has to be rebuilt, roughly once a decade. If you don't replace your own product, your competitors will.
Finally: skill, intellect and training matter, but they're not enough. You need passion for the painful parts of building a company. Which lesson do you need most? Tell me in the comments.
